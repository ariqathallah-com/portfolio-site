If he had to describe himself in one word, it would be called resourceful, or maybe cerdikiawan?

He really relates to one of the early Gojek ads called “[Cerdikiawan](https://youtu.be/viUwhsB00i8)”. It’s not a “real” word, but more like a pun, or a mix between cerdik (clever) and cendikiawan (scholar). It is a powerful word for him, and honestly, he thinks whoever made that ad deserves to be called cerdikiawan themselves.

The ad shows ordinary people working around on small everyday problems with whatever they have on hand. Whether it is about someone using a fork to fix a broken dispenser button, someone tying a strap around a plastic bag so the “kuah bakso” can stays separated at the bottom while the “bakso” sits on top, or about someone filling a large plastic bag with water and poking small holes in it and hanging it from the ceiling to make a DIY shower.

He wasn’t sure what the right English word for it was. Is it cunning, ingenious, or resourceful? But he thinks he would go with resourceful, or maybe “a resourceful scholar”.

I know this breaks the flow of the story, but bear with me, this part matters. There’s a line from [Steve Jobs’ 2005 Stanford commencement speech](https://youtu.be/UF8uR6Z6KLc) that stuck with me: “Today I want to tell you three stories from my life. That’s it. No big deal. Just three stories.” So here goes his three stories: no big deal, just three stories.

One of the first moments in his life that he can remember clearly happened is when he joined a drum extracurricular at 4th grade. He remember he couldn’t do the basic hi-hat, snare, and kick pattern. He almost always kicked again on the third beat, because his muscles were still a bit stuck since it was the first time I’d ever played drums. 

Then something came to his mind, “what if I just pressed my foot toward the kick on the third beat, but instead I moved it off the pedal?”. It ended up sounding good, his foot muscle still tapped the floor, but at least it wasn’t hitting the kick pedal anymore. He felt great. He could finally play the basic beat. 

Not sure whether it is not a good method or not (or maybe it is for a beginner?), but it felt great and he felt… cerdik, maybe?

The second event coming to his mind was when one of his friend just got an espresso machine. His friend was very excited to try the machine, and so is him. But the thing is, his friend forgot to buy the espresso shot glass, a small glass used to catch the coffee straight from the machine before it is poured into a final cup.

His friend actually already had a glass that caould replace the shot glass, but it was too tall for the machine cause they also needed to weigh the shot on a scale. And something came to his mind again instantly and told his friend, "Wait here, let me do something". Three minutes later, he gave him a can of soda that had been cut in half and goes "The glass was tall right? We can use this." 

"How do you even come up with stuff like this?" asked his friend, and there it went again, he felt somewhat cerdik.

The third and the last event he remember was ... [then the third event, idk what to tell in this part]

He probably should have ended his story the way Steve Jobs ended his speech, by looking back and explaining how these moments led to success, how “lucky” he was that the dots eventually connected. But he doesn't think he can do that, cause he is me, and I don't think I can do that, yet. This is still my first attempt at writing this down, and I haven’t lived far enough past these stories to know how they connect, or where they lead.

So I think I’ll just end it here for now. Thank you for reading this, and I really hope that one day, I can look back and “connect the dots” the way Steve did.
